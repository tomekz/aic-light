# Weekly Meal Plan

Use this template to turn one week's household schedule, food needs, pantry
stock, and budget into a coordinated meal plan and shopping list. It is
designed for four people but can be scaled for a different household.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Record allergies, intolerances, medical diets, and cross-contact risks before
> choosing meals. Check every ingredient label each time because products can
> change. For medical nutrition advice, use guidance from a qualified
> professional rather than this general planning template.

## Quick workflow

- [ ] Complete the weekly setup.
- [ ] Mark meals away from home and time-constrained days.
- [ ] Check the pantry, refrigerator, and freezer.
- [ ] Choose dinners first, then plan breakfasts, lunches, and snacks.
- [ ] Assign leftovers to a specific later meal.
- [ ] Add required ingredients to the shopping list and subtract stock on hand.
- [ ] Check the plan against the week's budget and available preparation time.
- [ ] Schedule advance preparation.
- [ ] After shopping, record substitutions and update affected meals.
- [ ] At the end of the week, note what to repeat, change, freeze, or use soon.

Before selecting meals:

- [ ] Ask each household member about schedule changes and meal needs.
- [ ] Check use-by dates and the condition of perishable food.
- [ ] Plan to use the most perishable suitable ingredients early in the week.
- [ ] Decide which days need quick, portable, make-ahead, or no-cook meals.
- [ ] Reserve one flexible meal for changed plans or ingredients that remain.
- [ ] Decide how many portions each meal needs; four people do not always mean
      four identical adult portions.

### Plan check

- [ ] Every meal reflects who will be present.
- [ ] Busy days have realistic preparation times.
- [ ] Household allergy and dietary requirements are covered.
- [ ] Planned portions match diners and intended leftovers.
- [ ] Perishable ingredients are reused across meals without excessive waste.
- [ ] Repetition is intentional and acceptable to the household.
- [ ] Backup food is available for one changed or failed meal.
- [ ] The estimated shop is affordable within the stated budget.
- [ ] Preparation and cleanup are distributed realistically.

When adapting a meal:

- Recheck labels and cross-contact information for every substitute.
- Keep a suitable component separate when that safely accommodates different
  preferences, such as serving sauce or toppings at the table.
- Scale recipe quantities using the servings needed, not household size alone.
- Treat online cooking times as estimates; confirm food is cooked safely.
- Avoid substitutions that conflict with a medical or allergy requirement.

### Shopping check

- [ ] Combine quantities used in more than one meal.
- [ ] Include staples, condiments, drinks, packed-meal supplies, and suitable
      backup food where needed.
- [ ] Do not buy an item already available in a usable quantity.
- [ ] Compare pack size with likely use so a saving does not become waste.
- [ ] Keep raw foods and ready-to-eat foods separated during transport.
- [ ] Put chilled and frozen items away promptly after shopping.
- [ ] Record any substitution that changes the meal plan or allergy check.

Useful preparation may include:

- Washing or cutting suitable produce.
- Cooking a grain, sauce, or base for more than one meal.
- Portioning snacks or packed lunches.
- Moving frozen ingredients to the refrigerator to thaw safely.
- Freezing portions that will not be used soon enough.
- Setting aside an intended leftover before serving the current meal.

## 6. Leftover and food-use tracker

Do not rely on memory. Label stored food, follow the product or recipe
instructions, and use local official food-safety guidance for storage,
reheating, and disposal decisions.

| Food | Prepared or opened | Storage location | Intended meal | Use or freeze by | Reheat or serving note | Used, frozen, or discarded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

- Cool, cover, store, thaw, and reheat food safely.
- Do not use smell or appearance alone to decide whether food is safe.
- Do not repeatedly reheat a shared batch; portion what is needed.
- When safety is uncertain, do not serve the food.
- Keep allergy-safe portions protected from cross-contact during preparation,
  storage, and serving.

## 7. Optional example for four people

This example demonstrates the workflow; it is not a prescribed diet or a
nutritionally complete plan. Adapt ingredients, portions, snacks, and recipes
to the household's needs, local guidance, budget, schedule, and available
food. Assume no allergies only for purposes of reading the example.

| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner | Snacks | Preparation and leftover plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Oatmeal with fruit | Hummus and vegetable wraps | Bean and vegetable chili with rice | Fruit; yogurt or suitable alternative | Make 8 chili portions; reserve 4 for Tuesday lunch |
| Tuesday | Yogurt or alternative with oats and fruit | Leftover chili and rice | Sheet-pan chicken or tofu, potatoes, and vegetables | Vegetable sticks; toast | Roast extra chicken or tofu and vegetables for Wednesday lunch |
| Wednesday | Eggs or suitable alternative on toast | Grain bowls with Tuesday's roast ingredients | Tomato and lentil pasta with salad | Fruit; popcorn | Make extra pasta sauce for Thursday dinner |
| Thursday | Overnight oats | Sandwiches and raw vegetables | Baked potatoes with leftover lentil sauce and toppings | Yogurt or alternative; fruit | Check remaining produce; freeze unused safe portions |
| Friday | Toast with nut or seed butter and fruit | Leftover baked-potato fillings in wraps | Build-your-own vegetable and bean tacos | Fruit; corn chips and salsa | Keep toppings separate; reserve suitable fillings for Saturday lunch |
| Saturday | Pancakes and fruit | Taco filling bowls | Vegetable soup with bread and a protein side | Smoothie; crackers | Make extra soup for Sunday lunch |
| Sunday | Eggs or suitable alternative with toast and fruit | Leftover soup and bread | Flexible pantry meal or planned meal away | Use-soon fruit; yogurt or alternative | Review leftovers; freeze or safely discard; note next week's carryover |

### Example coordination notes

- The chili, roast ingredients, pasta sauce, taco fillings, and soup each have
  a named second use; they are not unplanned leftovers.
- Breakfasts and snacks repeat to reduce shopping complexity.
- Friday's taco meal can keep components separate for differing preferences.
- Sunday's flexible dinner absorbs a changed schedule or suitable remaining
  ingredients.
- Protein and alternatives are written as choices because household dietary
  requirements are unknown; select only options appropriate for the household.
