# Household Subscription Review Checklist

Use this checklist to find, assess, and manage recurring household charges.
Review one subscription at a time and record only enough information to make
and track decisions.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Do not record passwords, full payment-card or bank-account numbers, security
> answers, recovery codes, or copies of identity documents here. Store
> credentials in a password manager. Use only a safe payment reference such as
> `Visa ending 1234`.

## Quick review workflow

- [ ] Choose the review period and set a completion date.
- [ ] Collect evidence from every discovery source below.
- [ ] Add each recurring charge to the inventory.
- [ ] Assign an owner and category to every item.
- [ ] Verify actual usage, price, billing frequency, and renewal terms.
- [ ] Flag duplicates, overlaps, price increases, and forgotten trials.
- [ ] Decide to keep, negotiate, downgrade, pause, share legitimately, or
      cancel each item.
- [ ] Complete each action and save confirmation details.
- [ ] Update the household budget and payment records.
- [ ] Schedule renewal reminders and the next full review.

## 1. Prepare

- [ ] Pick a lookback period:
  - At least 3 months for monthly subscriptions.
  - At least 13 months when possible, so annual renewals are included.
- [ ] Define who is included: household members, dependants, shared accounts,
      business reimbursements, and subscriptions paid by someone else but used
      by the household.
- [ ] Gather the latest household budget and any previous subscription list.
- [ ] Agree that the account owner must approve cancellations, plan changes,
      spending, or changes that could remove another person's access.
- [ ] Choose a temporary review status for each item: `researching`,
      `decision needed`, `action pending`, `complete`, or `no change`.

## 2. Discover every recurring charge

Search broadly. A charge may be annual, billed through an app store, bundled
with another service, or described under a parent company's name.

### Financial records

- [ ] Review credit-card statements for repeating merchant names and amounts.
- [ ] Review bank statements and direct debits.
- [ ] Review digital wallets and payment services such as PayPal.
- [ ] Review app-store and marketplace subscription pages.
- [ ] Check buy-now-pay-later or installment accounts for recurring services.
- [ ] Look for small charges, foreign-currency charges, and charges that occur
      every 4 weeks, quarterly, semiannually, or annually.

### Accounts and communications

- [ ] Search email for: `subscription`, `membership`, `renewal`, `trial`,
      `invoice`, `receipt`, `billing`, `price change`, `auto-renew`, `annual`,
      `monthly`, and `payment failed`.
- [ ] Check password-manager entries for services that may have paid plans.
- [ ] Review mobile-phone, internet, TV, utility, insurance, and banking
      accounts for paid add-ons.
- [ ] Check smart-TV, streaming-device, game-console, and mobile-app accounts.
- [ ] Ask each household member about memberships, school or children's apps,
      professional tools, clubs, donations, storage, and subscriptions they
      use but do not pay for directly.
- [ ] Check for free trials and promotional periods that will become paid.
- [ ] Check services included through an employer, school, bank, mobile plan,
      internet provider, credit card, or another subscription.

### Reconcile

- [ ] Match every statement charge to a known service and account owner.
- [ ] Investigate unfamiliar merchant names through the payment provider or
      official merchant support; do not use links in suspicious messages.
- [ ] Mark charges that cannot be identified for prompt follow-up.
- [ ] Confirm that annual subscriptions absent from recent statements are
      still captured from older statements or account pages.

## 3. Build the inventory

Copy this table into a dated review note, spreadsheet, or trusted budgeting
tool. Use one row per separately billed plan.

| Service | Category | Account owner | Users | Billing source | Safe payment reference | Current price | Billing cycle | Monthly equivalent | Next charge or renewal | Auto-renews? | Trial or promo ends | Cancellation notice/deadline | Actual usage | Decision | Action owner | Due date | Status | Confirmation/reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

For consistent comparisons:

- [ ] Convert every price to a monthly equivalent:
  - Annual: divide by 12.
  - Quarterly: divide by 3.
  - Weekly: multiply by 52, then divide by 12.
  - Every 4 weeks: multiply by 13, then divide by 12.
- [ ] Note taxes, foreign-exchange fees, service fees, and variable usage
      charges separately.
- [ ] Record reimbursements or credits so the household's net cost is clear.
- [ ] Record whether cancellation ends access immediately or at period end.
- [ ] Record the official cancellation method and any required notice period.

## 4. Categorize

Assign one primary category to prevent double counting:

- [ ] Entertainment: video, music, games, books, news, or events.
- [ ] Connectivity: mobile, internet, TV, domains, or communication tools.
- [ ] Household: security, maintenance, delivery, smart-home, or utilities.
- [ ] Health and fitness: gym, classes, wellness, or health apps.
- [ ] Food and shopping: memberships, meal kits, delivery, or product refills.
- [ ] Finance and insurance: account plans, monitoring, insurance, or advice.
- [ ] Productivity and cloud: software, storage, backup, or AI tools.
- [ ] Education and family: courses, childcare, school, or children's apps.
- [ ] Transport and travel: transit, roadside help, parking, or travel clubs.
- [ ] Professional: tools or memberships required for work.
- [ ] Donations and clubs: recurring giving, associations, or communities.
- [ ] Other: explain why it does not fit elsewhere.

Also tag each item as `essential`, `important`, `optional`, or `unused`, and
identify whether it is `individual`, `shared`, or `reimbursable`.

## 5. Check actual usage and value

Use evidence rather than memory where possible.

- [ ] Check account history, device usage, attendance, orders, downloads, or
      household-member feedback for the last 30, 90, and 365 days.
- [ ] Record the last-used date and approximate uses per month.
- [ ] Identify which listed users actually use the service.
- [ ] Calculate cost per use when meaningful:
      `monthly equivalent / average monthly uses`.
- [ ] Note benefits actually used, not every advertised benefit.
- [ ] Ask: What problem does this solve now?
- [ ] Ask: What would happen if it disappeared tomorrow?
- [ ] Ask: Is a free, owned, library-provided, employer-provided, or bundled
      alternative already available?
- [ ] Ask: Would the household subscribe again today at the current price?
- [ ] Mark seasonal subscriptions that should be paused outside active months.
- [ ] Mark services retained only because cancellation feels inconvenient.

## 6. Verify price and renewal terms

Use the provider's official account or terms, not an old promotional email.

- [ ] Confirm the current base price, taxes, fees, currency, and billing cycle.
- [ ] Compare the latest charge with the original or previous price.
- [ ] Check whether a discount, free trial, introductory rate, or credit ends.
- [ ] Confirm the exact next billing date and amount when available.
- [ ] Confirm whether renewal is automatic and whether the term changes at
      renewal.
- [ ] Check the cancellation deadline, notice period, minimum commitment, and
      early-termination fee.
- [ ] Check refund, prorating, pause, and cooling-off rules.
- [ ] Check whether cancellation removes stored data, loyalty benefits,
      purchase access, phone numbers, domains, or other assets.
- [ ] Check whether a monthly plan is safer than annual prepayment or whether
      annual billing has a worthwhile, affordable discount.
- [ ] Check current lower-priced tiers, retention offers, bundles, and
      eligibility discounts without starting a purchase.
- [ ] Capture a dated link or short summary of the verified terms.

## 7. Find duplicates and overlaps

- [ ] Group subscriptions by category and core purpose.
- [ ] Compare multiple video, music, cloud-storage, backup, security,
      productivity, delivery, news, fitness, and AI services.
- [ ] Check whether two household members pay separately for the same service.
- [ ] Check whether a legitimate family or household plan would cost less.
- [ ] Check whether a paid feature is already included in another plan,
      employer benefit, bank package, internet plan, or device purchase.
- [ ] Check for direct billing plus app-store billing for the same account.
- [ ] Check for an old account left active after changing an email address,
      device, provider, or plan.
- [ ] Distinguish genuine redundancy from deliberate backup or different
      household needs.
- [ ] Estimate the annual saving from removing each confirmed overlap.

Do not violate service terms by sharing individual plans, addresses, or
credentials with ineligible users.

## 8. Make a decision

Choose exactly one decision and record the reason.

### Keep

- [ ] Confirm it is used, affordable, and better than realistic alternatives.
- [ ] Confirm the current tier and billing frequency remain appropriate.
- [ ] Set a renewal reminder if the price or need should be reconsidered.

### Negotiate or switch

- [ ] Compare the provider's current public plans and legitimate competitors.
- [ ] Contact the provider through an official channel.
- [ ] Request an available loyalty, annual, bundle, or eligibility discount.
- [ ] Compare total cost, contract length, feature loss, migration effort, and
      cancellation terms before accepting.
- [ ] Do not accept a new commitment merely because the first month is cheap.

### Downgrade

- [ ] Identify the lowest tier that covers actual usage.
- [ ] Check storage, user, device, quality, feature, and support limits.
- [ ] Export or reduce data first if the lower tier has a smaller allowance.
- [ ] Confirm when the lower price and feature changes take effect.

### Pause or rotate

- [ ] Confirm pause duration, restart behavior, data retention, and price.
- [ ] Record a reminder before automatic reactivation.
- [ ] For replaceable entertainment services, consider keeping only one or two
      active at a time.

### Cancel

- [ ] Confirm the account owner and affected users approve.
- [ ] Back up or export required data, invoices, contacts, playlists, or files.
- [ ] Use the provider's official cancellation process.
- [ ] Decline unnecessary retention offers or new commitments.
- [ ] Record the cancellation date, effective date, confirmation number, and
      final expected charge.
- [ ] Save the confirmation in a secure household-records location.
- [ ] Remove related integrations, app permissions, and unused applications
      after needed data is secured.
- [ ] Check the next statement to confirm billing stopped.

### Decision test

- [ ] Is it essential or actively valuable?
- [ ] Is actual use sufficient for its annual cost?
- [ ] Is the tier sized correctly?
- [ ] Is there a cheaper legitimate way to meet the same need?
- [ ] Does it duplicate another paid or included service?
- [ ] Is an upcoming renewal, price increase, or lock-in changing the answer?
- [ ] Can the household comfortably afford it without displacing priorities?
- [ ] Has a named person accepted the action and due date?

## 9. Secure accounts and payments

- [ ] Use a unique password for every account and store it in a password
      manager.
- [ ] Enable multifactor authentication, preferably an authenticator app or
      security key, where available.
- [ ] Update recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
- [ ] Review active sessions, signed-in devices, connected apps, and authorized
      household members; remove anything unrecognized or no longer needed.
- [ ] Remove former household members or old employee/school access.
- [ ] Verify the billing address and safe payment reference.
- [ ] Remove expired or unnecessary payment methods.
- [ ] Avoid debit cards for merchants where a credit card or provider-issued
      virtual card offers better controls and dispute protection.
- [ ] Consider a dedicated payment method or virtual card for recurring
      charges if supported, but ensure essential services will not fail
      unexpectedly.
- [ ] Turn on transaction and renewal alerts with the provider, bank, or card
      issuer.
- [ ] Review data-sharing, marketing, privacy, and auto-renew settings.
- [ ] For an unknown or unauthorized charge, secure the account and contact
      the payment provider promptly rather than only cancelling the service.

## 10. Document completed actions

- [ ] Update the inventory with the final plan, price, billing cycle, next
      renewal, and status.
- [ ] Record savings as both monthly equivalent and annual amount.
- [ ] Update the household budget only after confirming the effective date.
- [ ] Store invoices, contracts, and cancellation confirmations in the
      household's secure records system.
- [ ] Record where the document is stored, not sensitive contents.
- [ ] Keep proof until at least the final expected statement has been checked
      and any dispute window has passed.
- [ ] Record unresolved disputes, refunds, credits, or follow-up dates.
- [ ] Tell affected household members what changed and when access ends.

## 11. Set reminders

- [ ] Create a reminder 30 days before every annual renewal.
- [ ] Create an earlier reminder when the contract requires more than 30 days'
      notice.
- [ ] Create reminders 7 days before trials, promotions, pauses, and temporary
      discounts end.
- [ ] Create a follow-up reminder after cancellation to verify that billing
      stopped and any refund arrived.
- [ ] Include the service, expected amount, decision deadline, account owner,
      and link to the official account page in each reminder.
- [ ] Avoid putting passwords, full account numbers, or payment details in
      calendar entries.

## 12. Repeat on a cadence

### Monthly: 10-minute charge check

- [ ] Scan statements for new, increased, duplicate, failed, or unfamiliar
      recurring charges.
- [ ] Confirm recent cancellations stopped billing.
- [ ] Add new subscriptions and trials to the inventory immediately.
- [ ] Resolve payment failures for essential services through official sites.

### Quarterly: focused review

- [ ] Review all optional and seasonal subscriptions.
- [ ] Recheck usage, overlaps, and plan sizing.
- [ ] Complete overdue cancellation, downgrade, and refund follow-ups.
- [ ] Confirm the inventory agrees with current statements.

### Annually: full household review

- [ ] Repeat every section of this checklist using at least 13 months of
      records.
- [ ] Reconfirm prices, renewal terms, security settings, account owners, and
      household needs.
- [ ] Compare total subscription spending with household priorities.
- [ ] Archive the dated review summary and schedule next year's review.

### Trigger an extra review when

- [ ] Household income or budget changes materially.
- [ ] Someone joins or leaves the household.
- [ ] A job, school, bank, mobile, or internet benefit changes.
- [ ] A payment card is replaced, lost, compromised, or expires.
- [ ] A provider announces a price increase, merger, plan migration, or terms
      change.
- [ ] The household moves, changes country, or changes key providers.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Every recurring charge found in the review period has a matching
      inventory entry or a documented explanation.
- [ ] Every entry has an owner, actual price, billing cycle, renewal date or
      best-known timing, usage assessment, and decision.
- [ ] Every cancellation or change has confirmation and a follow-up date.
- [ ] Security and payment hygiene checks are complete for retained accounts.
- [ ] The budget and review summary reflect confirmed changes.
- [ ] Renewal reminders and the next recurring review are scheduled.
